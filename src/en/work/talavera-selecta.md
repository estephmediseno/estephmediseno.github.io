<section class="wrap prose">
  <h2>The problem</h2>
  <p>A manufacturing plant is not a lifestyle brand. If social content only focuses on domestic cravings, commercial buyers find no operational rigor or logistical specifications. If the website only speaks in pallet specs, retail consumers feel no connection to the brand.</p>

  <h2>The system</h2>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Authentic factory backstage:</strong> content focused on production floors, on-camera packaging, and real food rather than polished stock footage.</li>
    <li><strong>Targeted cultural discussions:</strong> focused debates with high viewer retention (e.g. “queso al café”) instead of elaborate recipes that lose engagement.</li>
    <li><strong>Structured conversion channel:</strong> WhatsApp deployed as the direct communication point for commercial inquiries and orders.</li>
    <li><strong>Proprietary website (<a href="{{ m.handles.web }}">talaveraselecta.es</a>):</strong> complete information architecture, product catalog, factory overview, and wholesale section designed and developed from scratch.</li>
  </ul>
</section>

  <h2>Content patterns identified in reports</h2>
  <p>The reporting data clearly separates effective formats from failing ones:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>What works:</strong> manufacturing floor transparency, product close-ups, and cultural prompts that engage the Latin American diaspora in Spain.</li>
    <li><strong>What fails:</strong> long-form step-by-step recipes suffer steep retention drop-offs; generic open-ended questions on Instagram generate zero user comments.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>Commercial attribution</h2>
  <p>Four inbound direct messages were received in the first window and approximately five in the second regarding product purchases or distributor inquiries. As with Suitailor, the client reports do not attribute closed sales directly to social videos. The system’s role is driving brand recognition and funneling wholesale buyers toward the website and WhatsApp.</p>
